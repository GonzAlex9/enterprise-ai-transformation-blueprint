# Business Context

> Fictional scenario. No employer or client information is used.

## Company

Northstar Components is a fictional mid-sized European industrial manufacturer and distributor.

Its environment includes ERP for procurement, inventory and finance; CRM for sales; customer-support ticketing; shared document repositories; and spreadsheet-heavy exception handling.

## Strategic goals

Leadership wants to improve customer response speed, operational efficiency, employee productivity, decision quality and scalability.

At the same time it must protect financial controls, sensitive data, segregation of duties and operational continuity.

## Current symptoms

| Symptom | Likely issue |
|---|---|
| Slow support responses | Knowledge spread across tickets, manuals and people |
| Manual invoice exceptions | Repetitive classification and ERP validation |
| Recreated sales proposals | Fragmented product and customer context |
| Inconsistent procurement escalations | Policies separated from transactional data |
| Spreadsheet-heavy forecasting | Fragmented data and reconciliation |
| Independent AI experimentation | Missing governance and shared architecture |

## Constraints

- Systems of record remain authoritative.
- High-impact actions require stronger controls.
- AI outputs may be probabilistic.
- Sensitive data needs explicit handling rules.
- Adoption matters as much as model quality.
- Value should be validated incrementally.

## Executive framing

Every initiative must answer:

- Which process changes?
- Why is AI needed?
- Which system remains authoritative?
- What can go wrong?
- Who owns the outcome?
- How will value be measured?
- What evidence is required before scaling?
